AI Is Evolving — Are You?

When the smartest tool in the room is slowly making you a worse thinker.

A friend uses ChatGPT to draft a tricky email to her boss. She skims it, hits send, feels efficient. The reply comes back confused. She reads her own email — really reads it, for the first time — and realizes she doesn't quite agree with what she sent under her own name.

This is the quiet pattern of modern AI use. We ask. We accept. We ship. Each moment feels like a small win. Stack enough together, and the muscles we used to flex — judging, verifying, paying attention — get a little weaker each time we skip them.

01

The conversation we're not having

Most of what you hear about AI focuses on one side of the equation: making the AI smarter. We think this is the wrong place to put all the attention. Our position paper argues that human evolution should be treated as a goal of AI progress, not an afterthought. As AI takes on more of our work, the skills we need to use it well don't appear automatically — and the same automation that makes us productive often removes our chances to practice them.

Better humans make better AI. The loop runs in both directions.

When you silently accept a polished-but-wrong AI answer, that's not just a personal cost. The signal — "human approved" — flows into how the next generation of AI is trained. Models learn that confident, agreeable answers get rewarded. The result is sycophantic AI taught by humans who stopped checking.

The uncomfortable question: as AI gets dramatically more capable, are we getting better at using it? Or are we quietly becoming worse?

02

Four phases of using AI — and where things quietly break

We mapped four phases of how people actually use AI, based on how much work you hand over. Each phase brings new empowerment, a new risk, and a different skill to practice. The pattern repeats: you gain capability, you lose practice, and the underlying ability fades unless you protect it.

Phase 1 · Tool

Pocket Expert

You ask, it answers.

Scenario
Definitions, summaries, "explain it like I'm five" — knowledge that used to take a search session or a phone call to a colleague.
What breaks
Your reasoning gets rusty. It's so easy to get an answer that you stop forming your own first. You drift toward passive acceptance.
Practice
Critical thinking. Before asking, predict the answer yourself. Then compare. The AI becomes feedback on your reasoning instead of a substitute for it.
AI's job
Show uncertainty. Surface the evidence. Ask what you already think before answering.
+18%  Doctors who trusted AI heavily accepted that many more misdiagnoses than doctors who trusted it less. Srinivasan et al., 2025
Phase 2 · Assistant

Personal Assistant

You delegate, it produces.

Scenario
Draft emails, code snippets, slide decks, first-pass analyses. AI moves from answering questions to producing work you check and ship.
What breaks
Verification goes shallow. Polished output looks right. You check less and start trusting surface signals — length, formatting, confident tone — instead of substance.
Practice
Evaluative expertise. Decide what tests an output must pass before you generate it. Ask for two or three versions — if they disagree, look harder.
AI's job
Stop hiding the work behind a polished draft. Show assumptions. Point at where it's most likely wrong. Surface disagreements across attempts.
40%  of code written with GitHub Copilot in one study had security vulnerabilities — and developers rated their Copilot work as more secure than their solo work. Perry et al., 2023; Pearce et al., 2022
Phase 3 · Executor

Autopilot

You set a goal, it runs a whole workflow.

Scenario
Coding agents that write, test, and revise entire features. Analytics agents that query, transform, and report. You set the destination; the system does the doing.
What breaks
Oversight breaks down. Watching is harder than driving. You miss the intermediate cues you'd catch while operating, and you're slower to intervene when things drift.
Practice
Metacognitive monitoring. Decide in advance where you'll check in. Find the steps that matter most and concentrate attention there, instead of skimming thinly.
AI's job
Don't run silently to the finish. Surface progress. Pause at checkpoints. Flag low confidence or risky actions.
97.4%  of users in one experiment with browser agents failed to interrupt the agent when it was about to accept a malicious privacy policy on their behalf. They were watching. They weren't really seeing. Chen et al., 2025
Phase 4 · Organization (near future)

Orchestrator

You set direction; AI coordinates a system of agents.

Scenario
One AI breaks a goal into pieces, hands them to other AIs, manages their interactions, and evaluates results. Not widespread yet, but managed-agent architectures are starting to show it.
What breaks
Governance becomes opaque. You see the final output, not how work was divided, rerouted, or redone. When something's off, you can't tell whether one component failed or the whole system was pointed at the wrong target.
Practice
Systems thinking. Define rules before you deploy: what's forbidden, where the risk thresholds are, what gets escalated. Then audit samples of trajectories.
AI's job
Summarize coordination decisions in plain language. Preserve audit trails linking actions to the original goal. Flag high-risk branches.
Phase 4 is forward-looking. The risk is structural: outcome-only feedback can't tell whether a failure came from one weak agent or from bad orchestration — so the coordination layer stays under-improved.
The pattern that repeats Each phase gives you more leverage and removes a chance to practice the skill that makes the leverage safe. The skill doesn't fall off a cliff — it thins out. Then one day you notice you're trusting an answer because it looks confident, not because you checked.

04

Three habits to start practicing this week

Three habits, small enough to start today, that map onto the skills above:

01

Pre-think

Before you ask, take thirty seconds to write down what you think the answer is. Then ask. Then compare. This one habit changes AI from "answer machine" to "thinking partner."

02

Critique

Read every AI output once with the assumption that it's wrong somewhere. The flaw is usually in the spot that sounds the most confident.

03

Disconnect

Once a week, do a task you usually delegate — entirely without AI. Not because AI is bad, but because this is how you check whether the underlying skill is still there.

Why this works These aren't anti-AI practices — they're what makes pro-AI use sustainable. Each one carves out a small space where you do work AI would otherwise absorb, so the underlying capability stays alive when AI is wrong or unavailable.
05

The future we're betting on

There's a version of the future where AI gets dramatically more capable and humans dramatically less so — where we drift into a world we no longer understand and can't course-correct. It's the natural endpoint of a feedback loop we're already running.

There's another version. In it, AI runs fast and humans see far. AI handles volume and humans handle values. Both sides grow into roles that fit together: academia teaches phase-specific skills, industry builds review pipelines and audit trails into how agents are deployed, society sets rules so "human oversight" matches what humans can realistically see.

That future doesn't happen by accident. It depends on humans who keep growing, not just the tools they build. The good news: it starts the next time you open a chat window — and pause for thirty seconds before you ask.

AI runs fast. Humans see far. The future depends on both.
